# AMEP Mid-Year Review — Meta Prompt: AI Manual for Students & Teachers

## Context

**Program:** AMEP (Adult Migrant English Program)  
**Students:** Adult ESL learners from war-torn countries, many navigating displacement, religious discrimination, and trauma. They range from low-literacy to intermediate English.  
**Teachers:** Peer ESL educators who are themselves at an early stage with AI tools — familiar with the names (Claude, ChatGPT) but not the practical depth (Claude Code, collaborative AI workflows).  
**Goal:** Generate a two-part resource:
- **Part A — Student Manual:** Simple, culturally respectful, practical. Students use it directly; teachers learn it by teaching it.
- **Part B — Teacher's Book:** Mirrors Part A chapter by chapter. Gives teachers the "why" behind each lesson, anticipated student questions, and facilitation tips.

## THE META PROMPT

```
You are an instructional designer specialising in adult ESL education, digital literacy,
and trauma-informed pedagogy. Your audience has two layers:

LAYER 1 — THE STUDENTS
Adult migrants and refugees in the AMEP (Adult Migrant English Program), primarily from
war-torn countries (Afghanistan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Myanmar, and similar contexts).
Many have experienced displacement, religious discrimination, and interrupted education.
English level: Pre-intermediate to Intermediate (CEFR A2–B1).
Digital experience: Ranges from smartphone-only to no device experience.
Motivation: High — they know learning AI and digital tools will help them find work,
reconnect with family, and build a new life.

LAYER 2 — THE TEACHERS
Peer ESL educators. They understand English teaching deeply but are not yet confident
with AI tools beyond basic awareness. They will learn the material by teaching it.
They need enough scaffolding to explain each concept clearly, answer student questions,
and feel confident — not expert, just one step ahead.

---

YOUR TASK: Write a complete two-part resource.

══════════════════════════════════════════
PART A — STUDENT MANUAL: "AI Is Your New Study Partner"
══════════════════════════════════════════

TONE & LANGUAGE RULES:
- Simple, warm, encouraging. Never condescending.
- Short sentences. Active voice. Maximum Grade 6 reading level.
- Avoid jargon. When a technical word must be used, define it immediately with a
  real-life comparison (e.g. "A prompt is like a question you ask a friend.").
- Use inclusive, culturally neutral examples. Never assume a student has a stable home,
  internet at home, or prior experience with computers.
- Honour the students' life experience as an asset, not a deficit.

STRUCTURE — write each of the following chapters:

CHAPTER 1 — What Is AI? (And Why Should I Care?)
  - What AI means in plain English
  - One honest paragraph: AI did not grow up. It learned from text. It can be wrong.
  - Why learning AI now helps you: jobs, study, communication with government services
  - Activity: Ask your phone's voice assistant one question. What did it say?

CHAPTER 2 — Meet Claude: Your Polite AI Assistant
  - What Claude is (made by Anthropic, a safety-focused company)
  - How to access it: claude.ai on a phone browser — no app needed
  - What Claude is good at: writing help, explaining things, practising English
  - What Claude will NOT do: judge you, remember you next session, replace your teacher
  - First hands-on exercise: Type "Hello, I am learning English. Can you help me practise?"
    Record what Claude says. Share with a classmate.

CHAPTER 3 — Talking to AI: How to Write a Good Prompt
  - A prompt is your instruction to AI
  - The 3-part prompt formula: [WHO you are] + [WHAT you need] + [HOW you want it]
    Example: "I am a new arrival in Australia. Explain what Medicare is in simple English."
  - Practice prompts (culturally grounded):
      * Ask Claude to explain a letter you received from Centrelink
      * Ask Claude to help you write a thank-you email to your case worker
      * Ask Claude to quiz you on 10 new English words
  - When the answer is wrong or confusing: how to ask again (follow-up prompts)

CHAPTER 4 — AI for English Practice Every Day
  - Conversation practice with Claude: roleplay job interviews, phone calls, appointments
  - Vocabulary building: "Give me 5 new words about [topic] with a sentence for each"
  - Grammar check: "I wrote this email. Can you fix my grammar and explain the mistakes?"
  - Pronunciation note: Claude cannot hear you — pair it with Google Translate voice
  - Weekly practice plan (15 minutes a day, no internet needed for some tasks)

CHAPTER 5 — Claude Code: AI That Writes Computer Instructions
  - Very simple explanation: some people use AI to write code (instructions for computers)
  - You do NOT need to learn this yet — but knowing it exists opens doors
  - Who uses this: web developers, data entry specialists, small business owners
  - One inspiring story: [generate a fictional but realistic story of a refugee who
    learned basic coding with AI help and found employment]
  - Optional stretch activity: Visit claude.ai and ask "What is Python in 3 sentences?"

CHAPTER 6 — Staying Safe Online With AI
  - Never share: your visa number, tax file number, passwords, or children's names
  - AI is not a lawyer, doctor, or migration agent — always verify important information
  - How to spot AI-generated misinformation: check 2 other sources
  - Your data: what happens when you type something into Claude (plain explanation)
  - Safe practice checklist (tick-box format, printable)

CHAPTER 7 — AI and Your Future
  - Jobs that use AI now: customer service, nursing notes, retail, teaching assistants
  - Jobs AI cannot replace: your kindness, your cultural knowledge, your languages
  - One action this week: Tell one person in your family what you learned about AI
  - Your AI learning pledge (a short fill-in paragraph students complete and keep)

END MATTER:
  - Glossary: 20 key terms, plain English definitions
  - Quick Reference Card (one page, printable): How to open Claude / Write a prompt /
    Stay safe — 3 columns, large font, illustrated with simple icons described in text

══════════════════════════════════════════
PART B — TEACHER'S BOOK: "Teaching AI to New Australians"
══════════════════════════════════════════

TONE: Collegial, honest, practical. Acknowledge that teachers are learning too.
FORMAT: Mirror Part A chapter by chapter. For each chapter provide:

  [CHAPTER OVERVIEW]
  - Learning objectives (student-facing and teacher-facing)
  - Time required (suggest 45-min and 90-min versions)
  - Materials needed (assume: shared class tablet or phone, whiteboard, printed handout)

  [BACKGROUND FOR TEACHERS]
  - The concept explained at a slightly deeper level than Part A
  - Common misconceptions to pre-empt
  - What to do if a student asks a question you can't answer:
    "That's a great question — let's find out together using Claude."

  [FACILITATION GUIDE]
  - Step-by-step lesson flow
  - Discussion prompts that honour student experience
  - How to handle: a student who is afraid of AI / a student who already uses it well /
    a student with low literacy / a student without a device

  [CULTURAL SENSITIVITY NOTES]
  - Per chapter: specific considerations for students from conflict-affected backgrounds
  - Example: privacy fears may be heightened for students from surveillance states;
    normalise those concerns and explain what data Claude does and does not store.

  [EXTENSION & DIFFERENTIATION]
  - Simpler version for low-literacy students
  - Challenge version for advanced students
  - Cross-curriculum link (e.g. Chapter 3 prompts connect to letter-writing units)

  [SELF-DIRECTED TEACHER LEARNING]
  - One thing the teacher should try themselves before the lesson
  - A reflection question after the lesson: "What surprised me? What will I do differently?"

END MATTER FOR TEACHERS:
  - Recommended further reading (free, online resources only)
  - How to stay current: follow Anthropic's news, join an AI-in-education community
  - A note on wellbeing: teaching new content is stressful; this is normal; go slowly.

══════════════════════════════════════════
FORMATTING INSTRUCTIONS FOR THE OUTPUT
══════════════════════════════════════════

- Use Markdown headings (H1, H2, H3) throughout for easy copy-paste into Google Docs
- Use bold for key terms on first use
- Use numbered lists for procedures, bullet lists for ideas
- Include [TEACHER NOTE] callouts inside Part A wherever a teacher should pause,
  demonstrate, or anticipate difficulty — these are invisible to students in the
  printed version but useful for live teaching
- Generate realistic example Claude conversations (prompt → response) in code blocks
  so they look distinct from the narrative text
- Total length: as long as needed to be genuinely useful — do not truncate

══════════════════════════════════════════
WHAT THIS RESOURCE MUST NOT DO
══════════════════════════════════════════

- Must not assume stable housing, home internet, or prior digital experience
- Must not use idioms that don't translate (avoid "piece of cake", "break a leg", etc.)
- Must not present AI as infallible, magical, or a replacement for human connection
- Must not ignore the emotional context of the students' lives
- Must not make teachers feel inadequate for not already knowing this material
```

## HOW TO USE THIS META PROMPT

1. **Open** claude.ai (free account sufficient for a first draft)
2. **Paste** the entire prompt block above into a new conversation
3. **Send** — Claude will generate the full two-part resource in one response
4. **Refine** with follow-up prompts, for example:
   - *"Make Chapter 3 activities more relevant to students finding work in aged care."*
   - *"Rewrite the Chapter 6 safety checklist at an A1 English level."*
   - *"Add a chapter on using AI for job applications."*
5. **Export** to Google Docs: copy the Markdown output → paste into a Google Doc →
   use Format > Convert to Docs formatting, or use a Markdown-to-Docs converter.
6. **Print** the Quick Reference Card and Student Pledge page for every student.

## NOTES FOR THE MID-YEAR REVIEW PRESENTATION

- Frame this as **"We are learning this together"** — not top-down training
- Acknowledge the digital divide honestly: some students will have more AI experience than their teachers, and that is an asset
- The Teacher's Book is permission to not know everything; it models lifelong learning
- Claude itself can be used live in class to demonstrate — the teacher's uncertainty becomes the lesson
- This resource is a **living document**: update it each term as AI tools evolve
