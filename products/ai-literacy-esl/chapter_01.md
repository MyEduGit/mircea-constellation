# Chapter 1: What is AI?

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this chapter, you will:
- Understand what Artificial Intelligence (AI) means in simple words
- Know examples of AI you already use every day
- Feel confident talking about AI with others

---

## What is AI?

AI stands for **Artificial Intelligence**.

Let's break those two words down:
- **Artificial** = made by people (not natural)
- **Intelligence** = the ability to think, learn, and solve problems

So AI is a **computer program that can think and learn**.

But AI does not think like a person. It does not have feelings. It does not get tired. It looks at millions of examples and finds patterns. Then it uses those patterns to help you.

---

## AI You Already Use

You probably use AI every day without knowing it. Here are some examples:

| What you do | The AI behind it |
|------------|-----------------|
| Ask Siri or Google a question | Voice recognition AI |
| Use Google Translate | Language AI |
| Get Netflix recommendations | Recommendation AI |
| Unlock your phone with your face | Face recognition AI |
| Use autocorrect when typing | Text prediction AI |
| Get directions on Google Maps | Route planning AI |

**You are already an AI user.**

---

## How Does AI Learn?

Imagine you want to teach a child what a cat is.

You show them 100 pictures of cats. After seeing enough pictures, the child can recognise a new cat they have never seen before.

AI works the same way — but with millions of examples, not 100.

This is called **machine learning**. The computer learns from examples.

---

## AI is a Tool

Think of AI like a calculator.

A calculator does not replace a person. But it helps a person work faster and make fewer mistakes.

AI is the same. It is a **tool** that helps you:
- Write better English
- Understand documents
- Find information
- Practice speaking
- Fill out forms

**AI does not replace you. It helps you.**

---

## Key Words

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| AI | Artificial Intelligence — a computer that can learn |
| Machine learning | When a computer learns from examples |
| Tool | Something that helps you do a job |
| Artificial | Made by people, not natural |
| Pattern | Something that repeats in a regular way |

## Practice Activities

### Activity 1: Find the AI (Cert I)
Look at your phone. Find three apps that use AI. Write their names:
1. _______________
2. _______________
3. _______________

### Activity 2: AI or Not AI? (Cert II)
Read each sentence. Write YES if it is AI, or NO if it is not AI.

1. Google Translate translates a sentence for you. ___
2. You turn on a light switch. ___
3. Netflix suggests a movie you might like. ___
4. You use a paper dictionary. ___
5. Your phone corrects your spelling. ___

### Activity 3: Explain AI (Cert III)
In your own words, explain what AI is to a classmate. Use at least three sentences. You can start with:

*"AI stands for... It works by... An example is..."*

## Teacher's Note
This chapter introduces AI without jargon. The key message is that students are already AI users — this removes fear and builds confidence. Use the phone activity as a warm-up to show that AI is not new or scary to them.

Estimated class time: 45-60 minutes
Materials needed: students' phones, whiteboard
