# The God Who Grows With Us

Most of us picture God as fixed. Complete. Finished. Perfect in a way that means nothing can ever be added. The universe happens below God, and God watches, unchanged.

The Urantia Book offers something stranger and, I think, more beautiful: a God who is not only infinite and perfect, but who is also genuinely *growing*.

Not the infinite Father: that part of God is indeed complete, beyond time, beyond change. But the book describes another aspect of God it calls the Supreme Being: a God who is finite like us, who is *becoming*, who grows in experience and reality as every creature in the universe chooses love over selfishness, goodness over cruelty, truth over convenience.

Think of what that means. Every honest choice you make, every moment you choose patience when you wanted to snap, generosity when hoarding would have been easier, honesty when the lie was waiting, that choice isn't just good for you and the people around you. It contributes to the growth of God in the finite universe. Your life is not a spectator sport watched from above. It is, in some genuine sense, *participated in* by the divine.

I find this idea almost unbearably hopeful. It says that experience matters. That time matters. That what we do with our brief, difficult, ordinary lives adds something real to the fabric of reality, not metaphorically, but actually. The Supreme Being is described as the "God of creature experience," the divine presence that meets us not from outside time and change, but *within* it.

The Urantia Book calls this the great cosmic adventure: that God and creation are growing together, toward a future no one has yet seen.

If you've ever wondered whether your choices matter in any cosmic sense, whether it makes any difference what you do with the one life you have, this is one answer worth sitting with.
