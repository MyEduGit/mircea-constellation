# The Fatherhood of God and the Brotherhood of Man

If you asked me to compress the entire Urantia Book into a single phrase, its heart, the thing it most wants you to understand, I would not hesitate. It is this: the fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of man.

It sounds simple. It sounds, perhaps, like a slogan you've heard before. But the book means it with a depth and seriousness that, once I grasped it, reorganised how I see every other human being.

The fatherhood of God: not a distant deity, not a tribal god of one people, but the universal Father of all, every person, every race, every creature on every inhabited world. There is no one outside that fatherhood. No one God does not claim. No one carrying a fragment of the divine who is not, in the most literal sense, a child of God.

And from that follows, inescapably, the brotherhood of man. If God is genuinely the Father of all, then every human being you will ever meet is your brother or sister, not metaphorically, not as a nice sentiment, but as a fact of cosmic descent. The stranger, the enemy, the person whose politics or religion or behaviour you cannot stand: family. All of them.

Jesus, the book says, came to make this real, to take the abstract idea of God's fatherhood and live it so completely that no one could mistake it. His whole ministry, in the book's reading, was a demonstration of what it looks like to treat every person as a child of the same Father: the tax collector, the prostitute, the leper, the Roman, the Samaritan. No one outside the circle. No one beneath love.

This teaching judges me daily. It is easy to assent to it and hard to live it. But it remains, for me, the most quietly revolutionary idea I know. If we actually believed it, actually treated every person as a literal sibling under one divine Father, there is no social problem on earth that would survive the change.

That is what the book is for, in the end. Not cosmology for its own sake. Not theology as a puzzle. But this: a Father over all, and all of us, finally, family.
