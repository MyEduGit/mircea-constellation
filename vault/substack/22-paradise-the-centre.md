# The Seven Sacred Spheres of Paradise

When I first read the Urantia Book's description of the centre of all things, I had to put the book down and walk around the garden for a while. It is that big.

At the very centre of the universe of universes, the book says, is Paradise — not a planet, not a star, but the eternal, stationary Isle from which all reality flows. It does not orbit anything. Everything else orbits it. It is the literal and spiritual centre of infinity.

And on Paradise, the book says, dwells God — the Universal Father, the First Source and Centre — together with the Eternal Son and the Infinite Spirit, the three persons of the Paradise Trinity.

What stays with me is not the grandeur, though the grandeur is real. It is the claim that this centre — this source of all energy, all personality, all law — is *personal*. Not a force. Not a principle. A Father. A being who knows you, the book insists, by name; who is aware of the fall of every sparrow and the turning of every honest heart.

The book describes the journey to Paradise as the destiny of every surviving mortal — the long ascent I've written about before. But it also describes Paradise as the *home* we are all, slowly, making our way back to. Not a place we have never been, exactly, but a place we belong to by origin, because the fragment of God within us came from there and longs to return.

I find it steadying, in an anxious age, to hold this picture in mind. Beneath the chaos of the news, beneath the churn of ordinary worry, there is — if the book is right — an unmoved centre. A still point. A home that does not change, presided over by a love that does not fail.

You don't have to believe the cosmology to feel the pull of that. The longing for a centre that holds is one of the most human things there is.

The full text is free at [urantia.org](https://www.urantia.org). Papers 11 and 27 describe Paradise and its sacred spheres.
