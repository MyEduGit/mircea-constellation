# Is the Universe Friendly?

Einstein is said to have called this the most important question a person could ask. I don't know if the quote is accurate, but the question is real: is the universe friendly?

The two dominant modern answers are, roughly, yes and no.

Science says: the universe is neither friendly nor hostile, it's indifferent. The laws of physics apply to you exactly as they apply to a rock. Consciousness is probably an accident. Meaning is what you project onto a meaningless backdrop. There is no audience, no purpose, no one rooting for you.

Much of traditional religion says: the universe is friendly, but conditionally. God loves you, but you must believe the right things, perform the right rituals, belong to the right group. The friendliness is real but it has terms.

The Urantia Book offers a third position, and it's the one that has shaped my life more than almost anything else.

The universe, it says, is *genuinely* friendly, not conditionally, not metaphorically, but structurally. It is organised around personality, love, and the progressive discovery of truth. Every inhabited world is watched over. Every person is accompanied by an inner divine presence. The arc of the universe bends, slowly and sometimes painfully, toward light and life, a state of peace, beauty, and spiritual maturity that the book describes as the destiny of every world capable of reaching it.

This doesn't mean suffering is eliminated, or that the universe is fair in the short term. The book is honest about evil, pain, and the terrible cost of the gift of free will. It doesn't pretend that the friendliness is obvious from the outside. But underneath the difficulty, the evolutionary grinding of life on a world like ours, there is, the book insists, a bedrock of divine love that will not be removed.

I have tested that claim against my own seventy years. I have lived through losses that should have convinced me otherwise. And I keep arriving at the same place: not because the evidence is simple, but because something in me, that quiet fragment of God the book calls the Thought Adjuster, keeps pointing toward yes.

The universe is friendly. Not obviously. Not cheaply. But really.
