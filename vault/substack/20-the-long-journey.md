# The Long Journey Home

There is a journey described in the Urantia Book that I have never been able to stop thinking about, because it is — if the book is right — the journey every one of us is on.

It begins here, on this small and troubled world. It ends, after an almost incomprehensible traverse of time, space, and experience, in the presence of God at the centre of all things — a place the book calls Paradise.

The journey is not short. The book describes it in stages: the mansion worlds, then the broader constellation and superuniverse circuits, then the vast training worlds of the central universe, then finally the approach to Paradise itself. Along the way, a creature that began as a mortal on an evolutionary world gradually becomes — through choice, growth, and transformation — a being of such spiritual attainment that it can stand in the literal presence of the infinite God.

And there, the book says, the creature is received. Not as a servant, not as a subject, but as a *son* or *daughter* who has made the long way home.

What strikes me most about this picture is the patience of it. The journey takes aeons. There is no rushing, no shortcut, no moment when you are simply declared finished and done. Every stage is real; every experience adds something that nothing else could have added. The creature who arrives at Paradise is the same person who was born on an evolutionary world — continuous, recognisable, bearing the accumulated wisdom of an almost unimaginable journey — but also transformed beyond anything that original creature could have imagined.

The book calls this the eternal adventure. Not an ending but a beginning: because even after Paradise, there is more. The exploration of infinity never concludes.

I find this more sustaining than any promise of a static heaven. Not rest forever — though rest is available — but growth forever. Discovery forever. Deepening love, expanding understanding, new beings to meet and know, new beauty to encounter.

The universe, in this telling, is not a waiting room. It is a school, a home, and an adventure that has no final page.
