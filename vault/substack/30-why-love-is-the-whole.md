# Why Love Is the Whole of It

After all the cosmology — the millions of worlds, the orders of angels, the long ascent to Paradise, the architecture of a universe almost too vast to hold in the mind — the Urantia Book keeps returning to one small word, and insisting it is the largest thing of all.

Love.

Not love as sentiment. Not love as feeling. Love, in the book's sense, as the deepest reality there is — the very nature of God, the force that organises the universe, the law beneath all the other laws. "God is love," the book says, echoing the old scripture, and then it spends two thousand pages showing what that actually means across an entire cosmos.

What I have come to understand, slowly, is that for the Urantia Book love is not one virtue among many. It is the point of everything else. The vast machinery of the universe — the worlds, the journeys, the long evolution of souls — exists so that love can be learned, chosen, given, and returned. Free will exists so that love can be genuine. Suffering is permitted because a love that has never been tested is not yet fully real. The whole adventure, from the centre of Paradise to the smallest struggling world, is in the end a school of love.

And the measure of a life, the book says again and again, is not knowledge, not achievement, not religious correctness, not even belief. It is love. How much did you love? How well? How widely? How much of yourself did you give to the genuine good of others? That is the question that survives death. That is what the soul is made of.

Jesus, in the book's telling, came to demonstrate exactly this — that love taken to the very limit, love that forgives its executioners, love that lays down its life and asks nothing in return, is the strongest force in all creation and the clearest window we will ever have into the heart of God.

I am seventy years old, and I have read a great many things. I keep coming back to this. Strip away everything else — every doctrine, every cosmological detail, every fascinating digression — and what remains is the one instruction that contains all the others: love one another. The book makes that command vast, and roots it in the structure of reality itself.

That is why I keep reading. And it is why, in these short pieces, I keep writing to you.
