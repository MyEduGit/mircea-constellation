# The Real Cost of Free Will

If God is all-powerful and all-loving, why is there so much suffering in the world?

It is the oldest question. I've heard it asked in anger, in grief, in honest philosophical confusion. I've asked it myself, in moments when the world's pain felt too heavy to explain.

The Urantia Book has an answer. It is not a comfortable one, but it is, I think, an honest one.

Free will is real. Not metaphorically real, not "free within limits" in the way some theologians mean, but genuinely, dangerously real. God chose to create beings who could say no — to love, to truth, to goodness, to God — and mean it. That choice was not an oversight. It was the whole point.

The book explains it this way: a universe of beings who could only choose good would not be a universe of *persons*. It would be a very elaborate mechanism. God did not want mechanisms. God wanted sons and daughters — beings capable of genuine relationship, genuine love, genuine loyalty. And genuine love cannot be programmed. It can only be chosen.

The cost of that choice is everything we call evil. Not only the petty failures of ordinary selfishness, but the capacity for real cruelty, real destruction, the great catastrophes that human freedom has produced across history. The book doesn't flinch from this. It acknowledges that a rebellion — a real, cosmic rebellion — occurred in our part of the universe, and that its effects have reverberated through our world's history for hundreds of thousands of years.

But the book also says this: the suffering is not wasted. Not one moment of genuine pain is unobserved, uncompassioned, or without its possibility of producing something that could not have been produced any other way. Wisdom forged in suffering has a depth that untested wisdom never reaches. Compassion learned in loss has a quality that cannot be taught.

This is not the same as saying suffering is good. It isn't. It is saying that a God who gave us freedom accepted the consequences, stays present in every consequence, and is working — patiently, across enormous time — toward a universe where the gift of freedom eventually produces, in every willing soul, a love that was truly chosen.

That is either the most beautiful thing I've ever heard, or a story I've told myself to survive. After seventy years, I believe it is the former.
