# A Third Story

Most of us were handed two stories about where we came from. One from science, one from religion. For a long time I felt both left something out, neither quite explained who God is, or why we're here, in a way that satisfied me. Then I came across a book that offered a third picture: vast, strange, oddly coherent. I'm not asking you to believe it. I'm asking whether you've ever wanted a bigger answer than the ones on offer, because if you have, there's something I'd like to show you.

---

The book is called *The Urantia Book*. It arrived in 1955, and it doesn't fit neatly into any category. It isn't scripture in the way the Bible or the Quran is scripture. It isn't science fiction. It isn't philosophy in the academic sense, though it reasons carefully. It describes, in extraordinary detail, the organisation of the universe, the nature of God, the history of our world, and the long journey that every human being is on, whether they know it or not.

I'm 70 years old, and I've been sitting with these ideas for a long time now. I'm not a priest, a theologian, or a scholar. I'm just someone who found this third story and couldn't unfind it.

Here's one thing from it that has stayed with me. The book calls God the First Source and Centre, not a king on a throne, not an angry judge, but something closer to the ground of all reality: the source from which everything that exists radiates outward. And it says this: God is not a distant mystery to be feared, but a presence to be found, and the finding happens from the inside, not through institution or ritual, but through a genuine, honest desire to know.

That's a different claim than either story usually makes.

Science, at its best, says: here's how it works, the mechanisms, the timescales, the elegance of natural law. But it doesn't tell you why any of it exists, or why you should care about being good.

Religion, at its best, says: there is a why, and it matters deeply. But too often it asks you to accept answers rather than pursue questions, and to trust authority over your own inner life.

The Urantia Book does something unusual: it takes the scientific instinct seriously, the universe it describes is enormous, old, and law-governed, while also saying that meaning, love, and the search for God are not illusions, but the most real things there are.

I'm not asking you to accept that. I'm asking whether you've felt the gap between those two stories, and whether a third one is worth a look.

The full text is freely available at [urantia.org](https://www.urantia.org). If you're new to it, I'd start with the Foreword, it's dense, but it tells you immediately that this is something different. If the Foreword feels like too much, try Paper 1: "The Universal Father." Five pages. See what you think.

I'll be writing more here, short pieces, in plain language, about what I've found in this book and why I think it matters right now, when the world is looking hard for something to hold onto.
