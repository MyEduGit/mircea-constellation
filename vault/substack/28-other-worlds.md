# What the Urantia Book Says About Other Worlds

We live at a strange moment. For the first time in history, the question "are we alone?" is being asked by serious scientists with serious instruments, scanning the sky for any sign that someone else is out there.

The Urantia Book answered that question, in its own way, decades ago — and its answer is unambiguous. We are not remotely alone.

The book describes a universe teeming with inhabited worlds. Not a handful. Not thousands. Millions. Our local universe alone, it says, contains about ten million inhabited or inhabitable planets, organised into systems and constellations, each at its own stage of development. And our local universe is one of many.

But here is what makes the book's account different from ordinary speculation about aliens. It is not interested in spaceships, invasions, or little green men. It describes these worlds in *spiritual* and *developmental* terms — each one inhabited by will creatures who, like us, carry a fragment of God, who are on the same long journey toward Paradise, who are watched over by the same orders of celestial beings.

Most of these worlds, the book says, are further along than we are. We are, in fact, described as a relatively young, troubled, and spiritually disrupted world — quarantined for a time because of an ancient rebellion, only now beginning to recover. The other worlds are not our enemies. They are our elder brothers and sisters in a vast cosmic family, most of them living in a peace and beauty we can barely imagine.

I find this strangely humbling and strangely hopeful at once. Humbling, because it places our self-importance in perspective: we are one small, struggling world among millions. Hopeful, because it means our struggles are not unique, not hopeless, and not unwatched. Others have walked this road before us and reached the light. So, the book promises, will we.

Whatever the telescopes eventually find, the book's deeper claim stands: the universe is not empty, not silent, and not indifferent. It is full — of life, of purpose, and of family we have not yet met.

The full text is free at [urantia.org](https://www.urantia.org). Papers 49 and 50 describe the inhabited worlds and their development.

— M. G. Matthews
