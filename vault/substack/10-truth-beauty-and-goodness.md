# Three Words I'd Build a Life On

If you took the whole of The Urantia Book — two thousand pages, the cosmology, the history, the long life of Jesus — and boiled it down until almost nothing was left, I think you'd be holding three words.

Truth. Beauty. Goodness.

The book returns to them again and again, and treats them not as nice ideas but as the three faces of the divine that a human being can actually reach. You can't hold infinity in your hands. But you *can* recognise something true. You *can* be stopped in your tracks by something beautiful. You *can* know, in your chest, when something is good. The book's claim is that these three experiences aren't just feelings — they're the places where the divine touches ordinary life, the handholds by which a finite person climbs toward an infinite God.

What I love about this is how *practical* it makes the whole spiritual project. You don't need a temple or a doctrine or permission from anyone. You need only to do three things, as well as you can, in whatever life you've got: tell the truth and seek it; make and notice beauty; choose the good. That's it. That's the path, hidden inside the most ordinary day.

And the book says something I've found steadying: that these three always travel together, and that goodness is the one that completes the other two. You can have a clever truth that isn't good — and it curdles. You can have a dazzling beauty that isn't good — and it's hollow. But truth and beauty bound to goodness become something whole: a life that is honest, lovely, and kind, all at once. That braid is about the best description of a well-lived human life I know.

I'm 70, and I'll tell you what this has done for me. It has simplified things. When I'm unsure how to act, I don't reach for a rulebook anymore. I ask three small questions: Is it true? Is it beautiful? Is it good? Most of the muddle of a life clears up fast under those three lights. Not always — some choices stay hard — but more often than you'd think.

You don't need the book, or any belief about God at all, to try this. You already know what it is to be moved by truth, by beauty, by goodness. The book simply says: those moments aren't accidents or decorations. They're the most important compass you'll ever carry. Trust them. Follow them. They lead somewhere.

The full text is free at [urantia.org](https://www.urantia.org). The threefold path of truth, beauty, and goodness runs through the entire book like a thread.

— M. G. Matthews
