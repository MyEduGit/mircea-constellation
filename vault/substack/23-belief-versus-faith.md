# The Difference Between Belief and Faith

For most of my life I used the words *belief* and *faith* as if they meant the same thing. The Urantia Book taught me they don't, and the difference has mattered enormously.

Belief, the book suggests, is intellectual. It is the acceptance of a proposition as true. I believe the earth orbits the sun. I believe the book was published in 1955. Belief lives in the mind, and it can be argued into or out of with enough evidence.

Faith is something else entirely. Faith, the book says, is a living trust, a wholehearted reaching toward God that does not depend on proof and is not destroyed by doubt. It lives not in the intellect but in the soul. You cannot argue someone into faith, and you cannot quite argue them out of it either, because it is rooted in experience rather than evidence.

The book uses Jesus as the supreme example. His faith was not a set of beliefs about God. It was an unshakable, child-like trust *in* God, a confidence so complete that it carried him through betrayal, abandonment, and the cross itself. He did not have faith because he had proof. He had faith because he knew the Father, directly, in the depths of his own experience.

This distinction freed me. For years I had thought my doubts disqualified me, that because I couldn't *believe* every proposition with certainty, I had no business reaching toward God at all. The book says the opposite. Doubt is a function of the mind, and the mind is supposed to question. Faith lives somewhere deeper, and it can coexist with all the honest doubt in the world.

You can doubt and still trust. You can question every belief you hold and still reach, in the dark, for the hand you cannot see. That reaching is faith. And it is, the book says, enough.
