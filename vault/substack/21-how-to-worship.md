# How to Worship When You Don't Know How

I came to the Urantia Book as someone who had largely given up on formal religion. Not on God, I never gave up on God, but on the forms: the services, the creeds, the performance of certainty I didn't feel. And for a long time, I wasn't sure what to do with the instinct to worship that didn't go away just because I'd stopped going to church.

The Urantia Book addressed this more directly than I expected.

Worship, it says, is the most natural and highest activity of the soul. It is not a religious obligation. It is not a ritual. It is simply the soul turning toward God, honestly, quietly, without agenda, and resting there for a moment.

The book makes a distinction I have found enormously freeing: the distinction between *prayer* and *worship*. Prayer, it says, is a conversation, asking, expressing, petitioning, thanking. It flows from the creature toward God with a request or a need. Worship is different. Worship is not asking for anything. It is simply being in the presence of God, acknowledging what God is, and finding in that acknowledgement something that no other experience provides.

In worship, you are not a petitioner. You are a child, home.

The book says that worship is the one activity that is entirely about God rather than about you, and that in being entirely about God, it paradoxically does more for you than almost anything else. It reorganises the soul. It restores perspective. It makes the real things real again and the trivial things small.

You don't need a building for this. You don't need a liturgy, a priest, or a tradition. You need only the sincere desire to reach toward God from wherever you are, in whatever words come, or in no words at all.

I have sat with this on many mornings, usually before the household stirs. Nothing elaborate. Just attention, directed upward and inward. That quiet turning is, the book says, the most real thing a person can do.

If you've lost the forms but kept the instinct, you haven't lost anything essential. The instinct is the thing.

The full text is free at [urantia.org](https://www.urantia.org). Papers 5 and 91 deal most directly with worship, prayer, and the soul's approach to God.
