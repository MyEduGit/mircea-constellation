# The Soul Is Something You Build

I grew up assuming the soul was something you simply *had* — issued at birth, fixed, complete, waiting to be either saved or lost. The Urantia Book changed that picture for me completely, and I have never gone back.

In the book's account, you are not born with a soul. You are born with the *capacity* to grow one. The soul, it says, is something that comes into being over the course of a lifetime — a joint creation of the human mind and the indwelling fragment of God, the Thought Adjuster.

Here is how the book describes it. Every time you make a genuine moral choice — choosing kindness over cruelty, truth over a convenient lie, the higher path over the lower — that choice is, in a sense, recorded. The Adjuster takes the spiritual meaning and value of your decisions and weaves them into something new: a developing, immortal self that is neither purely material nor purely spiritual, but a growing reality in between. That growing reality is your soul.

This means your soul is, quite literally, the sum of your morally significant choices. It is built — slowly, decision by decision, the way a coral reef is built, the way a marriage is built, the way any real thing of value is built. Not granted. Not assumed. *Made.*

I find this idea both sobering and thrilling. Sobering, because it means nothing is wasted and nothing is hidden — every choice is doing quiet, permanent work on the self I am becoming. Thrilling, because it means I am not stuck. The soul I have at seventy is not the soul I had at thirty, and the soul I take into eternity is still, even now, under construction.

It also reframes the whole purpose of a human life. We are not here merely to be tested, or to accumulate, or to enjoy ourselves. We are here, the book suggests, to *build a soul* — to become, through the ordinary choices of an ordinary life, the kind of person fit to continue the journey beyond this world.

What are you building? The question is not rhetorical. According to the book, you are answering it every day, whether you mean to or not.

The full text is free at [urantia.org](https://www.urantia.org). Papers 111 and 112 deal with the soul, its growth, and the survival of personality.
