## T-2.1 · Teacher Background

### How does claude.ai work? What are students actually doing?

When a student navigates to **claude.ai**, they are accessing a web application
served by Anthropic's servers. No software installation is required. The chat
interface runs in any modern browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge).

**Free vs. paid:**
Claude.ai offers a free tier with a daily message limit (approximately 10–20
messages per day as of early 2024 ⏱). This is more than enough for classroom
practice. Students do not need to create an account to try the interface,
though creating an account allows saved conversation history.

**For classroom demos:** Your account is sufficient. Students observe in Module 2.
From Module 3 onward, students begin sending their own messages.

**Account creation:** If students want their own account, they need a valid email
address. The sign-up process is free and takes 2 minutes. You are not required
to facilitate this — students can also use the tool without logging in for basic
queries in many regions.

**Mobile note:** On some phones, claude.ai opens a prompt to install the Claude
app. The app and the website are functionally identical for classroom purposes.
Either is fine.

## T-2.2 · Before You Teach

- [ ] Open claude.ai on your device and confirm it loads in under 10 seconds.
- [ ] Test on a student phone (borrow one before class) to check the mobile layout.
- [ ] Know the address bar location on both Android (Chrome) and iPhone (Safari).
- [ ] If your classroom has shared laptops, open claude.ai on one and check layout.
- [ ] Print S-2.6 (fill-in-the-blank worksheet) as the offline fallback.
- [ ] Write **claude.ai** on the board in large letters — students will copy this.
- [ ] Have a second device charged if your demo device has battery issues.

**2-minute pre-flight:**
Open claude.ai → type "Hello. What can you help me with?" → confirm a reply arrives.

## T-2.4 · Scripts

### Opening

> "In Module 1 we learned what AI is.
> Today we are going to open Claude — the AI program — on our own devices.
> We will type a real question and read a real reply.
> I will show you each step on the screen.
> You copy the steps on your phone or computer.
> If something does not work, tell me. We fix it together."

---

### Writing the website address on the board

> "The website is **claude.ai**
> That is C-L-A-U-D-E dot A-I.
> There is no 'www' at the start. Just claude.ai.
> Write this in your notebook now."

*(Pause. Students copy.)*

---

### During the demo — narrate every click

> "I am opening Chrome on my phone.
> I am tapping the bar at the top. That is the address bar.
> I am typing: c-l-a-u-d-e dot a-i.
> I am pressing Go.
> The page is loading. I can see a white page opening.
> I can see a box at the bottom. It says 'Message Claude…'
> That is where I type my question.
> I will type: Hello. What can you help me with?
> Now I tap the arrow. That sends my message.
> I wait a few seconds.
> Claude is writing. I can see the dots moving.
> Now the reply has arrived.
> Let us read the first two lines together."

---

### When a student's device won't load the page

> "No problem. Stay with me on the screen for now.
> After class I will help you check your internet connection.
> You can still do the paper activity and the speaking task."

## T-2.5 · Differentiation

### Pre-A1

- Student does not type independently this lesson. They watch and point.
- Ask: "Can you find the box on my screen?" (Pointing is the skill.)
- Focus on the word **website** and the physical gesture of tapping a link.

### A1

- Follow the lesson as written.
- Allow students to use autocorrect when typing "claude.ai" — this is fine.
- If a student types a different question from the suggested one, this is excellent. Encourage it.

### A2

- Students type their own question (not the model question).
- Ask: "Did Claude understand you? How do you know?"
- Encourage students to read two or three lines of the reply, not just one.

### B1

- Students explore the interface independently after the demo.
- Ask: "What else can you find on this page? What buttons do you see?"
- Students try one follow-up question (a second message in the same chat).

## T-2.6 · Common Student Questions and Model Answers

**Q1: "Do I need to make an account?"**
> "No. You can use claude.ai without an account for basic questions.
> If you want Claude to remember your conversations, you need an account.
> Making an account is free. It only needs an email address.
> We will not do that today, but I can help you after class if you want."

**Q2: "My phone shows a button to download the app. Should I?"**
> "You can use either — the website or the app. They work the same way.
> The website does not take space on your phone.
> The app is easier to find later. Your choice."

**Q3: "The page is in English. What if I do not understand the buttons?"**
> "The main button you need says 'Message Claude…' — that is the box where you type.
> We will learn the other buttons in later lessons.
> For today, just find that one box."

**Q4: "Is this the same as Google?"**
> "Google is a search engine. It finds websites for you.
> Claude is a chat program. It writes answers for you.
> They are different tools. We will compare them in Module 6."

**Q5: "Can Claude remember me next time?"**
> "If you do not have an account, Claude forgets everything when you close the tab.
> If you create a free account and log in, Claude saves your conversations.
> Either way, be careful about what personal information you type."

## T-2.7 · Things That Will Go Wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Student types in address bar incorrectly | Common error: typing "claude.ai" in a Google search bar rather than the browser address bar. Say: "Look at the very top of the screen. There is a long bar. Tap there first." |
| Student gets a "sign up" wall | Some regions require an account. Pair the student with one who can access without sign-up, or use your teacher screen for the demo. |
| Student's phone has very small text | Show them: hold the page with two fingers and spread apart to zoom in. |
| Claude produces a very long answer in complex English | Reassure: "Claude sometimes writes a lot. We only need to read a little. Let us read just the first two lines." |
| Student accidentally starts a second conversation | Say: "That is fine. Each conversation starts fresh. There is no mistake here." |
| Student types personal information before you can stop them | Calmly: "Let us close that conversation and start a new one. Remember our safety rule — no real names or personal details." Open a new chat. Do not draw attention to the student in front of the class. |

## T-2.10 · Offline Plan B

If the internet is unavailable, run this activity:

1. Write on the board:

```
Student types:   Hello. What can you help me with?

Claude replies:  Hello! I can help you with many things.
                 I can answer questions about English.
                 I can explain words and sentences.
                 I can help you write emails and messages.
                 What would you like to know today?
```

2. Students copy the exchange into their notebooks.
3. Students underline every word they understand.
4. Students circle every word they do not know.
5. Class discusses the unknown words together.
6. Students write one question they would like to ask Claude in the next class.

## T-2.11 · Extension for Fast Finishers

1. Ask the student to send a second message to Claude — a follow-up question.
   Suggested follow-up: **"Can you say that in simpler words?"**
2. Student reads the new reply and decides: "Is the second reply easier to understand?"
3. Student writes two sentences comparing the two replies.

> This introduces the concept of **prompting** — asking AI to adjust its output.
> You can name this in B1 language: "We are giving Claude instructions."
