## T-7.1 · Teacher Background

### What is Claude for Chrome, and how does it work?

Claude for Chrome is a browser extension — a small add-on installed into Google Chrome.
Once installed, a Claude icon appears in the top-right corner of Chrome.
Clicking it opens a side panel where the student can chat with Claude
about the webpage currently visible in the browser.

**What it can see:**
- The text content of the active tab.
- Text the user highlights (selects).

**What it cannot see:**
- Other open tabs.
- Downloaded files on the device.
- Email inboxes, banking sites (blocked by those sites' security policies).
- Passwords, stored credentials.
- The camera or microphone (unless explicitly granted).

**Technical prerequisites:**
- Google Chrome browser (not Safari, Firefox, or Edge — this is Chrome-only). ⏱
- An Anthropic account (free tier is sufficient).
- The extension installed from the Chrome Web Store.
- Internet connection.

**iOS note:** Chrome extensions are generally not available on iPhones due to Apple's browser policy. ⏱ This lesson is primarily for laptop/Android users. iPhone users can follow the conceptual lesson and do the paper activity.

**Privacy note for teachers:** Claude reads the webpage to provide context. Students should avoid using this feature on pages that contain login forms, banking details, or sensitive medical records. This is covered in S-7.9 Safety Box.

## T-7.4 · Scripts

### Introducing browser extensions

> "Today we are going to add something to Chrome.
> It is called an extension.
> An extension is a small extra program inside your browser.
> It adds a new feature.
>
> The extension we are adding is Claude.
> After we add it, Claude will appear as a small icon in the corner of Chrome.
> When we click it, Claude opens on the right side of the screen.
> Then Claude can see what is on the webpage — and help us understand it."

---

### Explaining permissions

> "When you add an extension, the browser asks you to agree.
> This is called giving permission.
> The permission says: 'This extension can see the webpages you visit.'
>
> This sounds big — but it means: it can read the text on the page you have open.
> It cannot see your other tabs.
> It cannot see your email.
> It cannot see your bank account — even if you have another tab open.
>
> The most important rule: do not use Claude in Chrome when you are on a banking website.
> Close the Claude panel first."

---

### During the demo — narrate every click

> "I am opening Chrome.
> I am going to a health.gov.au page — an official page.
> This page has some long sentences.
> I am clicking the Claude icon in the top right corner.
> The panel is opening on the right.
> Now I will highlight this sentence. I click and drag over it.
> I type in the panel: 'Please explain this in simple English.'
> I press Enter.
> Claude reads the highlighted text and explains it.
> Let us read the explanation together."

## T-7.5 · Differentiation

### Pre-A1 / A1
- Student does not install the extension independently.
- Teacher demonstrates on the class screen.
- Student completes the paper plain-English activity (S-7.6).
- Student's target skill: understanding what an extension does.

### A2
- Student watches the installation demo.
- On a shared classroom laptop, student highlights one sentence and sends one Claude query.
- Student reads the explanation and circles words they understand.

### B1
- Student installs the extension on their own device (if Android/laptop).
- Student chooses their own hard webpage.
- Student asks two different questions about the same page.
- Student writes a comparison: "The page says… Claude summarised it as…"

## T-7.6 · Common Student Questions and Model Answers

**Q1: "I have an iPhone. Can I use this?"**
> "Unfortunately, Chrome extensions do not work on iPhones.
> This is an Apple rule — not a Claude rule.
> If you have a laptop or an Android phone, you can install it there.
> For today, you can do the paper activity and follow along on the class screen."

**Q2: "Can I install it on my work computer?"**
> "Some workplaces do not allow new extensions.
> If you want to try it at work, check with your IT department first.
> At home on your own device — yes, you can install it freely."

**Q3: "Will Claude save what I read?"**
> "Claude may store your conversations if you are logged in.
> It reads the text on the page to answer your question.
> It does not record your whole browsing history.
> For sensitive pages — banking, medical records — close the Claude panel first."

**Q4: "Can Claude fill in a form on a webpage for me?"**
> "Not automatically. Claude can tell you *what* to write in each field.
> But you type it yourself.
> This keeps you in control of your own information."

**Q5: "The extension is asking for permission to 'read all data on websites.' Is that safe?"**
> "This is standard language for browser extensions.
> It means Claude can read the page you are viewing — only when the panel is open.
> Anthropic (the company that makes Claude) has a privacy policy.
> For extra safety: only open the panel on pages you are comfortable sharing with Claude."

## T-7.7 · Things That Will Go Wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|------------|
| Extension installation fails | Try: disable any existing ad-blocker temporarily, refresh the Chrome Web Store page, ensure Chrome is up to date. |
| Student can't find the Claude icon after installation | Look in the Extensions puzzle-piece icon in the top-right corner. Click it, then pin Claude. |
| Claude panel opens but shows nothing | Refresh the webpage. Then click the Claude icon again. |
| Student accidentally opens Claude on their banking tab | Calmly say: "Close the Claude panel now — top-right X. Then close the banking tab. Let us open a new page." Do not draw attention to the student. |
| iPhone students feel left out | Actively include them in the paper activity. Say: "The concept is what matters — the extension is just one way to do it. You can do everything we practise in the app." |

## T-7.10 · Offline Plan B

Read aloud a complex government text (Appendix D-7). Students follow on their printed copy.
Class rewrites the text in simple English together on the whiteboard — one sentence at a time.
Each student writes their own plain-English version in their notebook.
Pair up: partners read each other's versions and ask: "Is anything missing?"

> "Find one webpage that is hard to understand.
> Open Claude in Chrome.
> Ask Claude to explain one paragraph.
> Write down what Claude said.
> Bring it to class."

> "Find one paragraph in a letter, a notice, or a magazine that you find hard to understand.
> Write down the words you do not know.
> Bring the paragraph (or a photo of it) to class.
> We will read it together."
