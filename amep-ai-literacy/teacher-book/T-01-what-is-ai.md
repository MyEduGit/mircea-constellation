## T-1.1 · Teacher Background

### What is AI? (Plain teacher English — no jargon assumed)

AI stands for **Artificial Intelligence**.
In everyday terms, it is a computer program trained on enormous amounts
of human-written text — books, websites, articles — so that it can predict
what a helpful response to any given question looks like.

The most important thing students need to know:
**AI is a program, not a person. It predicts words. It does not think.**

It does this impressively well, which is why it feels like talking to someone.
But it has no feelings, no memory of you between sessions (unless you create
an account and use saved conversations), and no way to verify facts.
It can be confidently wrong — this is called **hallucination**.

**Claude** (made by Anthropic) is the AI tool this manual centres on.
It is available free at **claude.ai** in a web browser and as a mobile app.
No download is required for the web version.

At this stage, students do not need to create an account.
You will demonstrate on your own device. They observe.

## T-1.2 · Before You Teach

### Setup Checklist — do this before class

- [ ] Open **claude.ai** in a browser on your device. Confirm it loads.
- [ ] Sign in to your own teacher account (free tier is sufficient for demos).
- [ ] Connect your device to the classroom projector or screen share.
- [ ] Test the projector. Can students at the back read the text on screen?
- [ ] Increase browser font size to 150% (Ctrl/Cmd + three times).
- [ ] Turn on Wi-Fi. Confirm speed is adequate (a text response loads in 3–5 sec).
- [ ] Print Student Book pages S-1.1 to S-1.11 for each student (or distribute digitally).
- [ ] Print the offline worksheet from Appendix D-1 in case internet fails.
- [ ] Have whiteboard markers ready to write the 5 key words.

**2-minute pre-flight test:**
Type *Hello. What is AI?* into Claude. Confirm you get a readable response.
If you do not, switch to the offline plan (see T-1.9).

## T-1.4 · Scripts

### Opening the lesson

> "Today we are starting something new.
> We are going to learn about AI.
> AI stands for Artificial Intelligence.
> Do not worry about those long words yet.
> I will explain everything step by step.
> You do not need to know anything about computers to start.
> Neither of us needs to be an expert. We will learn together."

---

### Introducing the 5 words (write each on the board as you say it)

> "Our first word is **AI**. We say it like two letters: *ay-eye*.
> AI is a computer program. It can read what you type and write back.
>
> Our second word is **chat**. Chat means to talk or write to someone.
> In this class, we will chat — write to an AI program.
>
> Our third word is **question**. A question is something you ask.
> When you type into AI, you type a question.
>
> Our fourth word is **answer**. An answer is what you get back.
> AI reads your question and writes an answer.
>
> Our fifth word is **screen**. The screen is the part of the
> phone or computer you look at. We will look at the screen together."

---

### Handling the question: "Is AI safe?"

*(This question will come up in Module 1 or Module 2. Use this script.)*

> "That is a very good question — and I am glad you asked it.
>
> AI is a program made by a company. Like any online service —
> email, Google, a bank app — the company can see what you type.
>
> So we have a simple rule: we do not type personal information.
> No full name. No address. No ID number. No medical history.
> We will look at this rule together on page S-1.9.
>
> For English practice, asking questions, and understanding things —
> AI is a useful tool. We just use it carefully."

---

### Closing the demo

> "You saw me type a question.
> You saw Claude write an answer.
> Claude is not a person. It is a program.
> It can make mistakes. We always check.
> In the next lesson, you will try it yourself."

## T-1.5 · Differentiation

### Pre-A1 (very early literacy / no prior digital experience)

- Sit beside the student during the demo. Point to the screen together.
- Skip the written activities. Use yes/no gestures and thumbs up/down.
- Focus only on three words: AI · question · answer.
- Use the picture-matching task from Appendix D-1 (circle the phone).

### A1 (beginner literacy, some phone use)

- Follow the lesson as written.
- Allow extra time on the fill-in-the-blank task (S-1.6).
- Pair with a slightly stronger student for the speaking task.

### A2 (functional literacy, regular smartphone use)

- Follow the lesson as written.
- Encourage students to write 2–3 sentences in S-1.8, not just one.
- Ask: "What other questions could you ask AI?"

### B1 (confident literacy, moderate digital literacy)

- Follow the lesson, then add the extension task (T-1.11).
- Ask the student to explain AI to a Pre-A1 partner in simple words.
- Encourage them to find one example of AI they already use (maps, autocorrect).

## T-1.6 · Common Student Questions and Model Answers

**Q1: "Do I need to pay for AI?"**
> "Claude has a free version. For today's lesson, I will show you on my screen.
> In Module 2 we will look at how to open Claude on your own phone — for free."

**Q2: "Can AI speak my language?"**
> "Yes. Many AI programs can chat in many languages.
> In this class, we use AI in English — to practise English.
> But if you are stuck, you can type in your language and it will understand."

**Q3: "Is AI smarter than a person?"**
> "AI is very fast at finding information. But it does not understand life.
> It cannot feel, it cannot make real decisions, and it can be wrong.
> A doctor, a teacher, or a lawyer uses their experience and judgment.
> AI cannot replace that."

**Q4: "Can AI watch me through my phone camera?"**
> "No. Claude cannot see your camera or your photos unless you share them yourself.
> We will not be sharing photos in this class.
> The program only reads what you type."

**Q5: "What company makes Claude?"**
> "Claude is made by a company called Anthropic.
> It is an American company.
> They have rules about how Claude can be used.
> Claude will not help with anything dangerous or illegal."

**Q6: "My friend uses ChatGPT. Is that the same?"**
> "ChatGPT and Claude are similar tools. They are both AI chat programs.
> ChatGPT is made by a different company called OpenAI.
> In this class we use Claude, but the skills you learn work for both."

## T-1.7 · Things That Will Go Wrong (and How to Recover)

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|------------|
| Projector does not connect | Switch to offline plan B. Draw a phone screen on the whiteboard. Write a fake "AI conversation" on the board as a model. |
| Claude takes more than 30 seconds to respond | Say: "Sometimes AI is slow, like a website. We wait. This is normal." Count aloud together — students practise numbers. |
| Claude gives a very long answer that confuses students | Say: "AI sometimes writes a lot. We only read the first part today. That is enough." Scroll to show only the first paragraph. |
| A student shares personal information during a practice task | Calmly say: "Thank you — let us keep our real names private in AI tools. Let us use a made-up name instead." Do not make the student feel embarrassed. |
| A student has no device and cannot follow along | Pair them with another student. Say: "You will share and take turns." All independent tasks have paper alternatives. |
| Student asks a political or religious question about AI | Say: "That is a big question. Let us keep today's focus on how AI works. You can write your thoughts down and we can talk after class." |
| Wi-Fi drops completely | Move to the paper version of S-1.6. Students complete the fill-in-the-blank without a device. Vocabulary and speaking tasks need no internet. |

## T-1.9 · Digital Literacy Rubric — Module 1

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| **Emerging** | Student can identify the screen and chat box when pointed to. Can respond yes/no to questions about AI. Needs physical guidance to engage with the device. |
| **Developing** | Student can locate the chat box independently. Can write or dictate one question. Understands that AI is a program, not a person. |
| **Consolidating** | Student can explain what AI does in one or two sentences. Can identify what not to type (personal information). Asks follow-up questions about AI. |

## T-1.10 · Offline Plan B

**If Wi-Fi fails entirely:**

1. Write this on the board:
   ```
   You:     Hello. What is AI?

   AI:      Hello! AI is a computer program. It can read
            what you write and write back to you. It learns
            from lots of books and websites. I am an AI.
            I am not a person. Is there something I can help
            you with today?
   ```

2. Students read the "AI reply" aloud in pairs — one student reads "You", one reads "AI".
3. Continue with all paper-based activities (S-1.6, S-1.7, S-1.8).
4. Nothing in Module 1 is lost by going offline — the concepts are fully covered in print.

## T-1.11 · Extension for Fast Finishers

Ask the student to:

1. Think of three questions they would like to ask AI.
   Write them in their notebook.
2. Look at the questions and ask: "Is any of my information in these questions?"
   If yes, rewrite the question without personal information.
3. Share one question with the class. The class votes: "Safe to ask?" or "Not safe?"

## T-1.12 · Homework / Try at Home

**If the student has a device:**

> "Before next class, open a browser on your phone.
> Go to **claude.ai**
> Type this question: **What is AI?**
> Read the answer.
> Write down one word from the answer that you do not know.
> Bring it to class."

**Paper alternative (no device needed):**

> "Before next class, find the word **AI** somewhere — on TV, a sign, a magazine, a phone advertisement.
> Write down where you found it.
> Bring the word (or a photo of it) to class."
