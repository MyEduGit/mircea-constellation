## T-8.1 · Teacher Background

### What is "Cowork" in the context of this curriculum?

"Cowork" is used in this curriculum as a general term for any workflow where a student
uses Claude to interact with files and documents — uploading them, pasting text from them,
or describing their contents. This includes:

- Uploading a PDF or Word document to Claude and asking questions about it.
- Copying and pasting text from a form into Claude.
- Typing out a question from a form and asking Claude to explain it.

Claude.ai supports file uploads (PDF, Word, text, images) on both free and paid tiers,
with some size and format restrictions. ⏱

**Why this module matters for AMEP students:**

Adult migrant learners encounter a high volume of forms:
enrolment forms, health forms, tax forms, housing applications, consent forms, Centrelink paperwork.
The language in these forms is often dense bureaucratic English.
Claude can serve as an on-demand plain-English explainer.

**The consent checkpoint:**
This module introduces the idea that uploading a file is an act of consent.
The student is choosing to share that document's contents with an external service.
Build this as a reflective habit: before uploading, ask "Would I be comfortable
if someone I don't know could read this?" If no — don't upload. Type the question instead.

## T-8.4 · Scripts

### Introducing the lesson

> "In today's lesson, we are going to use Claude to help us understand forms.
> Forms are everywhere — at the doctor, at school, at work.
> They ask questions. Sometimes the questions are hard to understand.
> Claude can explain the questions in plain English.
>
> Today I want to show you three ways to share a document with Claude.
> Each way is useful. But each way has different privacy considerations.
> We will talk about privacy before we practise."

---

### The consent checkpoint — building the habit

> "Every time you share a file with Claude, I want you to ask yourself one question.
> I will write it on the board.
> [Write: 'Would I be comfortable if a stranger could read this?']
>
> If the answer is yes — it is probably fine to share.
> If the answer is no — type the question instead. Do not paste or upload the document.
>
> Your passport copy — no.
> Your Medicare card — no.
> Your bank statement — no.
> A question from an enrolment form — yes, that is fine."

---

### Modelling Method 1 (typing the question — safest)

> "I have a form here. [Hold up the sample form.]
> Question 5 says: 'Previous formal qualifications obtained in Australia or overseas.'
> I do not want to upload the whole form.
> So I just type the question into Claude.
> I type: 'Please explain this form question: Previous formal qualifications obtained in Australia or overseas.'
> Watch what Claude says."

## T-8.5 · Differentiation

### Pre-A1
- Student does not interact with Claude independently.
- Teacher reads a form question aloud. Student listens to Claude's explanation.
- Student activity: match form questions to simple English meanings from a printed list.

### A1
- Student chooses one question from S-8.6.
- Teacher helps type it into Claude. Student reads the reply.
- Student writes the meaning in their own words (one sentence).

### A2
- Student independently types two form questions into Claude.
- Student writes the Claude explanation in their own words.
- Student discusses with a partner: "What would you write for this question?"

### B1
- Student uploads a real form (with personal info removed or blacked out).
- Student asks Claude to explain the entire form section by section.
- Student writes a personal answer for each question (using made-up details).
- Extension: student compares two different forms and identifies similarities.

## T-8.6 · Common Student Questions and Model Answers

**Q1: "Can Claude fill in the form for me?"**
> "Claude can tell you *what* to write. But you write it yourself.
> You know your information — Claude does not.
> Claude explains the question; you give the answer."

**Q2: "I uploaded my form and Claude can see my name. Is that a problem?"**
> "It is better practice to remove your name before uploading.
> But if it is already done, the conversation is private to your account.
> For next time: black out or replace personal information before uploading.
> We practised the safer method — typing the question — so you have that option."

**Q3: "What does 'cultural background' mean on a form?"**
> "This question is asking where your family comes from.
> It is usually optional — you do not have to answer it.
> Organisations use this information for planning — to understand who they serve.
> You can write your country of origin, or write 'prefer not to say.'"

**Q4: "What is the difference between 'legal name' and 'preferred name'?"**
> "Your legal name is the name on your passport or ID documents.
> Your preferred name is what you like people to call you.
> For example: your legal name might be a long name, but you prefer a shorter version.
> Write the legal name on official forms. You can also add the preferred name if there is a box."

**Q5: "I have a form in another language. Can Claude read it?"**
> "If you can take a photo of the form and upload it, Claude may be able to read it.
> Claude can read many languages.
> But for important official forms, also ask a settlement worker or your teacher to check."

## T-8.7 · Things That Will Go Wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|------------|
| Student uploads a document with full passport details | Calmly: "Let us close this conversation. We will not use that upload. Next time, let us black out personal information first." Start a new chat. |
| File upload fails (wrong format or too large) | Switch to Method 1 or 2. Say: "We will type the question instead — it is actually safer." |
| Claude explains a form question incorrectly | Treat as a teachable moment: "Let us check this with another source. I will look it up now, or you can ask a settlement worker." |
| Student's form is in a language Claude cannot read | Suggest having the form translated first, or asking a bilingual community worker. |

## T-8.10 · Offline Plan B

Use a printed copy of the sample form (Appendix D-8).
Students complete the form with made-up information.
For each question they find difficult, they write a question mark in the margin.
Class discusses each question mark together. Teacher explains in plain English.
Students write the plain-English meaning next to each difficult question.

## T-8.11 · Extension for Fast Finishers

1. Student collects three forms they have encountered in Australia.
   (Enrolment, health centre, library, post office — any common form.)
2. Student finds the hardest question on each form.
3. Student asks Claude to explain each question.
4. Student writes a "form survival guide" for someone who is new to Australia:
   "When you see this question, it means…"
