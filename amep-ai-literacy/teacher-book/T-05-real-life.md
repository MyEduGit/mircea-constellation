## T-5.1 · Teacher Background

### Using Claude as a writing scaffold and document decoder

This module has two core functions:
1. **Email drafting:** Claude can produce a fluent, polite draft email from a simple instruction. Students supply the intent; Claude supplies the formal language. The risk is that students adopt Claude's draft wholesale without editing or understanding. Build in a verification step — students must read the draft aloud and change at least one word before "owning" it.

2. **Document comprehension:** Students often receive letters, bills, and notices that use bureaucratic or legal English. Claude can "translate" these into plain English reliably and quickly. This is one of the most immediately useful skills in the AMEP context.

**Verification principle:** Introduce the word *verify* in this module and return to it throughout the course. The checking habit — "Claude helped me write this; before I send it, I will re-read and confirm the facts" — is the most important meta-skill this curriculum teaches.

**Privacy scaffolding:** This is the module where students are most likely to paste real personal documents into Claude. Build the privacy replacement habit explicitly: before pasting any text, remove or replace names, account numbers, dates of birth, and addresses with placeholders.

## T-5.4 · Scripts

### Introducing the document comprehension task

> "Have you ever received a letter that you did not understand?
> Maybe from your electricity company, your doctor, or the council.
> The words are English, but they use long, complicated sentences.
> Today, Claude is going to help us read those letters.
>
> I have a letter here. [Hold up the sample letter.]
> I will copy a part of it and paste it into Claude.
> I will ask: 'Please explain this in simple words.'
> Watch what happens."

---

### Introducing the email drafting task

> "Now we will do the opposite.
> Instead of understanding a letter, we will write one.
> Claude will help us write a polite, formal email.
>
> Here is the important rule: Claude writes a *draft* — a first attempt.
> We must read it. We must check the facts.
> Then we change the details to match our real situation.
>
> Claude does not know your real name, your real account number, or your real dates.
> You add those at the end.
>
> Think of Claude as a translator from your idea to formal English."

---

### The verification step — making it a class habit

> "Before anyone sends an email that Claude helped write:
> Step 1: Read the whole email aloud.
> Step 2: Check every fact — name, date, amount, account number.
> Step 3: Ask yourself: does this sound like something I would say?
> Step 4: Change one sentence to make it more like your voice.
>
> If you are not sure, show it to me before you send it.
> I will check it with you."

## T-5.5 · Differentiation

### Pre-A1
- Teacher reads the sample letter aloud. Student listens.
- Teacher types the document into Claude together. Student watches.
- Activity: student points to one word in Claude's plain-English reply that they understand.

### A1
- Student reads a short (2-sentence) extract from a letter.
- Teacher helps them type the prompt. Student reads Claude's reply.
- Student completes the fill-in-the-blank email template from Appendix D-5.

### A2
- Follow the lesson as written.
- Student drafts an email from Situation A or B, reads the draft, and changes at least two details.

### B1
- Student writes a full email (5–8 sentences) using Claude as a checker, not a drafter.
- Student writes the email themselves first, then asks Claude: "Is this formal? Is it clear? Can you improve it?"
- Student compares the original and Claude's revision and explains what changed.

## T-5.6 · Common Student Questions and Model Answers

**Q1: "Can I just send the email Claude wrote without changing it?"**
> "You can — but I recommend you change it a little.
> The email should sound like you, not like a computer.
> At minimum, add your correct name, account number, and date.
> And read it to make sure everything is accurate."

**Q2: "What if the company replies and I don't understand the reply?"**
> "Bring the reply to class. We can use Claude to help explain it.
> You can also call the company and ask them to explain it on the phone.
> You have the right to ask for plain English."

**Q3: "Can Claude write a letter to Centrelink or Medicare?"**
> "Yes. Claude can help you draft a letter to any organisation.
> Always replace personal information with placeholders when practising.
> Before you send the real letter, check the facts yourself."

**Q4: "What if the information in Claude's reply is wrong?"**
> "This can happen with bills and documents.
> Claude reads what you paste — it cannot see the full document.
> Always go back to the original letter to check amounts, dates, and account numbers.
> Claude gives you the *meaning*. The facts are in the original document."

**Q5: "I need to write to my landlord. Can Claude help?"**
> "Yes. This is a common and very useful use of Claude.
> We will practise landlord letters in the extension activity.
> Use a made-up address in your practice."

## T-5.7 · Things That Will Go Wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Student pastes a real letter with their name and address | Calmly stop the activity. Say: "Let us remove your real information first. We will use a made-up name." Do not embarrass the student. Close the tab and start a new chat. |
| Claude produces an email that is too formal or unusual | Say: "Claude sometimes writes very formal English. That is fine for official letters. Let us check the key words." Read the email aloud together. |
| Student cannot find a letter to use | Use a sample letter from Appendix D-5. Every student has a copy. |
| Student sends the email before verifying | This is a learning moment. Say: "What would you check before sending a real email? Let us look at the draft together and make a checklist." |

## T-5.10 · Offline Plan B

Print the sample letter from Appendix D-5.
Students read together and answer comprehension questions:
1. How much money is owed?
2. By what date?
3. What happens if you don't pay?

Students then write their own formal email using the fill-in-the-blank template in Appendix D-5. No Claude required.

## T-5.11 · Extension for Fast Finishers

1. Student chooses a real organisation they need to contact (council, Medicare, school).
2. They explain their situation to Claude in plain words.
3. Claude drafts a formal email.
4. Student reads, edits, and brings the final version to the teacher for feedback.
5. Discussion: "What did Claude get right? What did you change?"

## T-5.12 · Homework / Try at Home

**With a device:**
> "Find a letter or bill at home.
> Find one sentence you do not understand.
> Type it into Claude (without your personal information).
> Ask: 'What does this sentence mean?'
> Write down what Claude tells you. Bring it to class."

**Paper alternative:**
> "Find a letter or bill at home.
> Underline one sentence you do not understand.
> Bring the letter to class. We will read it together."
