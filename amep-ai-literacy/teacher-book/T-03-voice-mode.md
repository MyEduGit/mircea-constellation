## T-3.1 · Teacher Background

### How does voice input work in Claude?

There are two distinct voice pathways students may encounter:

1. **Phone keyboard microphone (universal):** Every modern smartphone keyboard has a microphone icon. Tapping it activates the phone's built-in speech-to-text engine (Google or Apple). The transcribed text appears in Claude's message box. The student then taps Send manually. This is available on *any* website or app — no special Claude feature required.

2. **Claude app voice mode:** The Claude mobile app (iOS/Android) has a built-in voice conversation mode. This is more conversational — the student speaks, Claude responds audibly. This feature may require a free or paid account and may vary by region. ⏱

**Accent tolerance:** Claude's underlying voice recognition is generally good across many accents, but will sometimes misrecognise heavily accented words. This is a genuine teaching moment — students can observe when the phone misunderstands them, retype the correct word, and learn that technology is imperfect.

**Pronunciation coaching:** Claude can describe how to say a word (stress patterns, syllables) in text but cannot play audio. For audio pronunciation, direct students to dictionary apps (Macquarie, Cambridge) that include audio buttons.

## T-3.4 · Scripts

### Introducing voice mode

> "Today we are going to talk to Claude — not type.
> We will use our voice.
> This is called voice input.
> Your phone can listen to you and write what you say.
> Then we send the message to Claude the same way we always do.
>
> This is very useful for English practice.
> We can ask Claude: how do I say this word?
> Claude will tell us the correct pronunciation."

---

### When voice input misunderstands a student

> "Did the phone write the wrong word? That happens.
> The phone did not understand your accent.
> This is not your fault.
> English has many accents — Australian, American, British, Indian, African.
> The phone learns from all of them, but it is not perfect.
> Let us type the correct word instead. Now Claude has it right."

---

### Handling anxiety about speaking aloud in class

> "I know speaking in class can feel uncomfortable.
> You do not have to speak loudly.
> You can lean close to your phone and speak quietly.
> Or you can type the question instead — both ways work."

## T-3.5 · Differentiation

### Pre-A1
- Student does not use voice input independently. They listen to the teacher use it.
- Activity: Student points to a word on the pronunciation list. Teacher speaks it into the phone. Student repeats what they hear from Claude's explanation.

### A1
- Follow the lesson as written.
- Student chooses one word and practises it three times.

### A2
- Student chooses three words and writes the pronunciation guide for each.
- Student teaches one word to their partner.

### B1
- Student records their own voice (phone voice memo), listens back, then asks Claude: "Is this how you say [word]? Let me describe how I said it: [description]."
- Student compares Claude's guide to their own recording.

## T-3.6 · Common Student Questions and Model Answers

**Q1: "My phone does not have a microphone on the keyboard."**
> "Check the bottom of the keyboard — look for a small microphone icon.
> On some phones you need to enable it in keyboard settings.
> If you cannot find it, we can type the question instead. Both work."

**Q2: "Claude speaks back to me — is that normal?"**
> "Yes. The Claude app has a voice reply feature.
> Claude will speak its answer aloud. You can also read it on screen.
> You can turn the sound off if you prefer."

**Q3: "Will Claude tell me my accent is wrong?"**
> "No. Claude will tell you the standard pronunciation of a word.
> It will not say your accent is wrong.
> Accents are natural and normal.
> Pronunciation practice helps you be understood — not to erase your accent."

**Q4: "Can I practise a whole conversation in English with Claude?"**
> "Yes. You can type or speak sentences and Claude will respond.
> This is good practice. Just remember the safety rules — no personal information."

**Q5: "Can Claude understand my language?"**
> "Yes — Claude understands many languages.
> But in this class we practise English.
> If you get stuck, you can type in your language to check your understanding."

## T-3.7 · Things That Will Go Wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|------------|
| Microphone permission denied | Say: "Your phone needs permission to use the microphone. Go to Settings > Apps > Chrome (or Safari) > Permissions > Microphone > Allow." |
| Phone mishears every student | Switch to typing for this lesson. The typing prompt works identically. |
| Student speaks too quietly and phone doesn't hear | Suggest: "Hold the phone about 10 cm from your mouth. Speak a little more clearly — not louder, just clearer." |
| Room is too noisy for voice input | Suggest paired voice practice in the corridor, or switch to typing. |
| Claude app voice mode not available in region | Use keyboard microphone method instead. Both achieve the same result for this lesson. |

## T-3.11 · Extension for Fast Finishers

1. Student finds 5 words from a real document (a letter, a bill, a health card) that they find hard to pronounce.
2. They ask Claude for the pronunciation of each.
3. They write a personal pronunciation guide in their notebook.
4. They practise each word and teach one to the class next lesson.
