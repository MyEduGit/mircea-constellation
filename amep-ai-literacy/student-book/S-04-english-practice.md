## S-4.4 · Look and Listen

🔊 **Listen. Follow with your eyes.**

---

**Dalia wants to write a message to her doctor. She asks Claude to check her English.**

> **Dalia types:** Please check my sentence. Is this correct?
> "I need make appointment next week."
>
> **Claude replies:** Good try. Here is a small correction:
> "I need to make an appointment next week."
> Two changes: add "to" after "need," and add "an" before "appointment."
>
> **Dalia types:** Why do I need "to"?
>
> **Claude replies:** In English, after the word "need," we usually add "to" and then a verb.
> Example: "I need to go." "I need to call." "I need to eat."
>
> **Dalia types:** Can you give me more examples with "need to"?
>
> **Claude replies:** Of course!
> "I need to buy bread."
> "I need to pay the bill."
> "I need to speak to the manager."

---

**Read again. Circle the right answer.**

1. Dalia asks Claude to **check / translate** her sentence.
2. Claude adds the word **"to" / "the"** after "need."
3. Claude gives Dalia **one / three** more examples.

*(Answers: 1. check · 2. "to" · 3. three)*

## S-4.5 · Do It With Me

👀 **Watch. Then do together.**

### Useful questions to ask Claude:

**To check a sentence:**
> "Please check my sentence. Is this correct? [your sentence]"

**To learn a word:**
> "What does the word [word] mean? Give me an example."

**To ask for simpler words:**
> "Please explain that in simpler words."

**To ask for more examples:**
> "Can you give me three examples?"

**To ask for easier text:**
> "Can you write that for someone learning English? Use short sentences."

---

### Try it now:

1. Open Claude on your device.
2. Type one sentence you want to check. It can be:
   - A sentence from your homework.
   - A sentence you wrote today.
   - A sentence you want to use at work or at a shop.
3. Before your sentence, type: **"Please check my sentence:"**
4. Send it.
5. Read Claude's reply.
6. Ask one follow-up question.

## S-4.9 · Safety Box

🔒 **When you practise English with Claude:**

- You can use made-up names. Write "Anna" or "John" — not your real name.
- You can describe a made-up situation. Write "my friend needs a doctor" instead of using real details.
- Do not share your address, visa number, or medical information — even in a practice sentence.

> **Good practice sentence:** "My friend Anna needs to make a doctor appointment."
> **Not safe:** "I, [your real name], living at [your address], need a doctor for my [medical condition]."
